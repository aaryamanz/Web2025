1

one template, their exact words

I used the same resume template for every application. The only part I changed was the top third, and I rewrote it using the exact words from the job description.

No new resume per company. No three-hour rewrites. One solid base, and a focused rewrite of the part a recruiter actually reads first.

only apply where you already match something

Before you tailor anything, check that the posting has at least two or three requirements you can honestly point to: a course, a project, a co-op, a club role. If you can't find any, that posting isn't the best use of your evening. Tailoring works by moving your real experience to the front, not by inventing new experience.

read the posting in three passes

  1. Split it. Required qualifications on one side, nice-to-haves on the other. Required items go in your top third. Nice-to-haves go wherever they fit.
  2. Mark the nouns. Tools, methods and deliverables, like Excel, SQL, stakeholder management or financial models. Those are the words you're going to mirror.
  3. Find what they repeat. If a phrase shows up three times, that's what the hiring manager actually cares about. Your first bullet should answer it.

what "the top third" means

A recruiter's first pass is a skim, and it starts at the top. On a one-page resume, the top third is your education details and your first experience entry. That's what I rewrote every time:

  • Certifications and coursework. Swap in the ones closest to the posting.
  • Your first role. Reorder the bullets so the closest match comes first, and use the posting's phrases wherever they're true.
  • Your numbers. Bold the ones this employer cares about.

Then a two-minute bonus: reorder your skills line so the tools they listed come first. See it marked up on the template.

write every bullet the same way

action verb+what you did+how big+what changed
BeforeHelped with process improvement projects on the operations team.
AfterMapped the client onboarding workflow with 6 teams and cut turnaround from 9 to 5 business days.
BeforeResponsible for creating reports in Excel.
AfterAutomated a weekly exceptions report in Python and Excel, saving the team about 6 hours a week.

The one rule: only mirror words that are true. If the posting says SQL and you've never written a query, leave it out. Anything on your resume is fair game in the interview.

2

apply within 48 hours

I applied within 2 days of every posting going live, so my resume was in the first batch a recruiter opened.

Many teams start screening as applications come in, and some postings close early once they have enough applicants. Being early is the cheapest edge there is. It costs nothing but attention.

set this up once, before postings open

  • LinkedIn job alerts. Search the role and city, for example "new grad analyst Toronto", then turn on the alert and set it to daily.
  • Careers-site alerts. Most big firms post through a careers portal, and many let you create a job alert. Make one on every firm on your list.
  • Company pages. Follow each firm on LinkedIn and turn on notifications.
  • A target list. 15 to 30 firms, written down, with a link to each careers page.
  • Your base resume, already in the template, so tailoring takes 30 minutes instead of a whole night.

I also keep new grad and internship deadlines on the 2027 page.

the 48-hour routine

  1. Hour 0The alert lands. Open the posting and save the full job description text somewhere. Postings get taken down, and you'll want it again before the interview.
  2. Within 24 hoursRun it through the matcher, rewrite your top third, export the PDF. Aim for 30 to 45 minutes.
  3. Within 48 hoursSubmit. Log it in the tracker. Send the analyst message if you haven't already.

Saw a posting late? Apply today anyway. 48 hours is the target, not a cutoff.

3

message one analyst first

Before applying, I messaged one analyst at each firm. Not to ask for a referral, just to connect and ask how the team actually works.

A stranger asking for a referral reads as a transaction. A stranger asking a good question starts a conversation, and it gives you something real to say when the interviewer asks why this team.

who to message

  • An analyst or associate one to three years in, on the team you're applying to. Not a recruiter, not a director.
  • Alumni from your school first. A shared school is the easiest reason for someone to accept.
  • One person per firm. More than that starts to look like you're working the room.

how to find them

  1. On LinkedIn, search the firm name plus "analyst", then filter to People.
  2. Filter by current company, then by your school.
  3. Open a few profiles and pick someone whose team matches the posting.

when to send it

Ideally a week or two before postings open, so the conversation is already going. If the posting is already live, send the message the same day and still apply within 48 hours. Don't wait for a reply before you apply.

use what they tell you

Whatever they say about the team goes into your "why this firm" answer, and into your cover letter if there is one. Something like: "When I spoke with an analyst on the team, she mentioned the first year is mostly client reporting, which is the exact work I did at my co-op." Ask before you use their name.

the resume template

This is the exact format I used, filled with mock values so you can see how each part should read. The highlighted band is the top third, the only part I rewrote for each application.

No LaTeX experience needed. Overleaf is free and runs in your browser: open the template, edit the text, then select Recompile to get your PDF.

  1. a
    Certifications and courseworkSwap in the ones closest to the posting. A data role gets Power BI and statistics first. An audit role gets accounting first.
  2. b
    First bullet of your first roleLead with the experience that answers the posting's top requirement, in its exact words if they're true. If they say "stakeholder management", you say "stakeholder management", not "worked with teams".
  3. c
    Bold the number they care aboutSavings for a cost-focused role, volume or users for a product role, hours saved for an operations role.
  4. d
    Skills, outside the top thirdReorder so the tools in the posting come first. Two minutes, no rewriting.

section by section

Header

Name, city, phone, email, LinkedIn, and a website if you have one. No photo, no full street address.

Education

School, degree, dates, and GPA if it helps you. Then three lines: awards, certifications, coursework. Only show grades next to courses when they're strong.

Experience

Most recent first. Two to four bullets per role, every one with a number in it.

Projects and ventures

Anything you built or ran: apps, research, case competitions, stock pitches. Name the tools you used.

Leadership

Club and volunteer roles with an outcome attached. A title on its own doesn't say much.

Skills

Grouped by type and reordered for each posting, so the tools they named come first.

    questions people ask

    What if nothing on my resume matches the posting?

    Look wider than jobs: a project, a course, a case competition, a club role. Your top third can lead with a project if it's the closest match. If there's still nothing, skip that posting and put the time into one where you do match.

    Should I just copy the job description into my resume?

    No. Mirror the phrases that describe work you've actually done, in the places where they're true. A person reads your resume after any screening software does, and keyword stuffing is obvious to a person.

    The posting has been up for a week. Is it too late?

    Apply today. Early is better, but plenty of postings stay open for weeks. 48 hours is the target, not a cutoff.

    Do I need to know LaTeX?

    No. Open the template in Overleaf and edit the words between the brackets. If you'd rather use Google Docs or Word, copy the structure: same sections, same order, one page.

    Does this work for internships too?

    Yes. The three steps are the same. Swap "new grad" for "internship" in your alerts and messages.

    What if the analyst never replies?

    Nothing changes. You already applied within 48 hours. Some people reply weeks later, and a few never do. Don't send more than one follow-up.
